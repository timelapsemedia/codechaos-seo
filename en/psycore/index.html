Genre guide · Psycore

What is Psycore?

Psycore is the fastest and most extreme subgenre of psytrance: 180 to over 300 BPM, mostly between 190 and 240 BPM. It fuses the dark, hypnotic sound worlds of darkpsy with the force of hardcore. Typical elements are minimal sine-wave kicks, rolling neurotrance basslines built from very fast note divisions, and dense, static soundscapes that pull you into a trance rather than inviting classic dancing.

By Code Chaos (Tim Borchert), producer from Hamburg · updated 5 Oct 2026

Origins

Psycore grew out of two directions: darkpsy, which pushed psytrance towards horror, night and the occult, and hitech, which kept raising tempo and detail density. When producers started pushing past 200 BPM while radically stripping back the percussion, a distinct sound appeared. Around 2010 the name psycore became established for it.

The scene is small but very international and loyal. Most releases come out on Bandcamp and small netlabels that often release psycore, hitech and forest side by side. Producers and parties are especially active in Latin America, Europe and Asia.

What does psycore sound like?

Psycore works differently from most dance genres. Instead of build-ups, breaks and drops, an almost unchanging pulse sits at the centre while layers of sound slowly shift above it. It feels more like a state than a song.

  • Kick: a short, almost pure sine wave. At 250 BPM there is no room for long tails; every millisecond of release costs punch.
  • Bass: the "neurotrance" bassline, a rolling figure of very fast notes that rumbles rather than grooves.
  • Percussion: deliberately sparse, often without any snare or clap. The space belongs to the atmosphere.
  • Atmosphere: dense, reverb-heavy pads, drones, vocal fragments and glitches, with clear dark ambient and noise influences.
  • Arrangement: long and repetitive with minimal variation. Psycore wants to hypnotise, not surprise.
"Psycore isn't a genre you listen to. It's a state. 300 BPM isn't a speed, it's a frequency."Code Chaos

Psycore, hitech or darkpsy?

The three genres overlap in tempo but differ in character. Darkpsy (150–200 BPM) is atmospheric, occult and comparatively accessible. Hitech (170–230+ BPM) is complex, glitchy and unpredictable and can even sound euphoric. Psycore (180–300+ BPM) is the most extreme: minimal, static, dark and hypnotic.

Producing psycore: notes from the studio

Code Chaos has been producing psycore, hitech and darkpsy since 2016. In our experience, these points make or break a psycore track:

  1. Kick first, and keep it short. A sine kick with a fast pitch drop and a very short release. At 240 BPM there are only 250 ms between two hits.
  2. Give the bass its own slot. The neurotrance bassline lives between the kicks. Precise per-note volume shaping often works better than classic sidechain compression.
  3. Clean lows, dirty mids. Keep sub and kick mono and clean and build character in the mids. Multiband saturation such as Crucible is made for this: hard clip on the low band, tube on the mids.
  4. Layer the atmosphere. Three to five pad and drone layers with different movement work better than one big pad.
  5. Master with respect for transients. At high tempo, too much limiting destroys the kick. Genre-aware psycore mastering keeps loudness and punch in balance.

FAQ: Psycore

How fast is psycore?

Psycore runs from 180 to over 300 BPM. Most tracks sit between 190 and 240 BPM, and some experiments go up to 400 BPM.

Is psycore the same as speedcore?

No. Speedcore comes from hardcore techno and is driven by rhythm and distortion. Psycore comes from psytrance and lives on hypnotic soundscapes, neurotrance basslines and minimal percussion, even if the tempo can be similar.

Where can I find psycore music?
